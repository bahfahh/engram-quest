# 05 Ways of showing: what has been done (examples, not a menu)

The catalogue is organised by the layers of `03-adapter-pattern.md` §1: §3 stages and whole-middle methods (layer 1), §3b segment shapes (layer 2), §3c moment devices (layer 3), §3d links between segments and the ending. Most of §3 comes from **one course** — a course for an indie app developer validating an app with a small app-install ad budget. It is an operate-a-tool subject, so real-interface methods dominate; later experiments added physics (Atlas), biology and abstract mechanisms (zoom out / zoom in). **For another subject, the learning principles and `03-adapter-pattern.md` §2 are a better start than this table**. If something here fits, use it; if it fits only loosely, design your own.

## 1. Status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `approved` | The user reviewed it and found it good. Approved methods coexist; none replaces another |
| `trial` | Made, not yet reviewed by the user — evidence of an idea, not a rule |
| `rejected` | The user reviewed it and did not pick it. Write which content it was used for — the content can still be taught another way |
| `needs rework` | Direction approved, execution not |

Only the user's review changes a status: the user judges whether a finished film works.

**Free and Pro** (`11-teachers.md` §1b; the user's product decision): everything here is available to every teacher unless marked **Pro**; the Pro marks are the curiosity-and-depth devices that only Pro teachers (Nova, Professor Bao, Luna, Iris, Alden) use. Nico (free) keeps the full floor. Approved qualities for all teachers (how the aha is composed, only the change moves, the student's reaction, the recap that draws the path, carrying the object across a seam, recap-card words as an anchor, a wrong proposal answered by its right part first, quiet sound effects, closing a segment at its reveal) live in `04-skeleton.md` §2 and §6; the cue that travels a relation, two rulers and measured-and-true on one dial are in §3c, the bet on the blackboard in §3b, the map that asks the previous lesson in §3d.

**Approved means available, not required.** An approved method, shape or device is used where the segment's diagnosis calls for it (`03-adapter-pattern.md` §1) and left out where it does not fit; no lesson has to contain all of them, and none is a quota per segment. They are not rules: the situation decides, as the adapter pattern says, and a device used because it exists is force-fitted.

**Approvals are of methods, not of a look.** Many methods below were tested in films shot in the Engram studio (the lesson-6 builds named below: x22u, x27, x30r, x30u, x32n), so their examples show each method through studio parts: lights down, holo panels and the glass vault, the house colours (cyan, amber), station chips, the wipe. What was approved is the method — the sound, the bet, the cue, the recap; timing, order, what moves, what is said — and it applies unchanged back in the classroom. So the useful thing to take from those builds is their structure and timing, rebuilt in the lesson's own classroom and stage; their studio parts belong to a film the user asked to shoot in the studio (`01-principles.md` §1).

### Notes on the approved methods
- **Real interface**: easy to fall into the same shell every lesson; interface text in wide shots gets too small; the student shrinks to a jobless speech bubble. Components: `lib/kit/` (phone, feed, Ads Manager pieces, callouts, camera) — Meta-ads specific; build the equivalent for your subject's real screens.
- **Whiteboard**: modelled on a clear reference film (Google Cloud Tech, "AI Agent Memory Architecture": very clear, one idea at a time). Clean means few things, not small things — the one idea reads best filling the board. A split screen's other half earns its place by doing work (a case panel that changes with each idea, or a map of the decision flow lighting the current step); lesson 7's puppet sat idle. Implementation: one track per element (appear, vanish, move, draw line, stamp), `render(t)` a pure function of t (the render contract); DOM pages render cheaply (≈0.2–0.5 s per frame).
- **Atlas diagram world** (`lib/atlas/ATLAS.md`): plan the camera beats, the lit table and the explode moments in the understanding card before building. What worked: one colour per quantity for the whole film; tip-to-tail adding and cancelling as separate steps; drawn dimension lines to measure. It replaces only the middle: head and tail stay the classroom skeleton the user values. For single-concept lessons the course map can be a three-panel blackboard (already know → today → later), each panel held ≈ 3 s or more so it can be read.
- **Zoom out / zoom in decomposition** (experiment 18c). It fits a mechanism with no real picture; with a real screen, real code or a real organ, the real thing, focused, usually teaches better (`01-principles.md` §1 "Every frame has a focus"). Few-shot: three demos — A card payment (2 frames + mindmap), B fees and compounding (2 + 1), C GPS (4 + 1); stills `assets/looks/6a–6d`, and the counter-example `6x`. References for a way of thinking, not templates; take what fits:
  - **Focus by size, position and contrast**: the subject is large and central; one accent colour means one thing (C: orange = the error only; B: orange = the gap only; A: purple = claim data going out, green = money coming back). Everything else stays in dark ink, clearly visible, rather than greyed to nothing.
  - **One sentence states the frame's point or question** ("If the phone's clock is off, every distance is off with it", "The same 1 million, 1.42 million apart after 30 years"); a heading, a spoken question or a picture that says it alone can all work.
  - **The picture runs the mechanism with recognisable objects** (satellite → dashed line → phone with a stopwatch reading 0.07 s; two circles crossing at two dots; store, bank buildings, coins), drawn large with solid fills — not 24 px outline icons.
  - **Real numbers and honesty labels stay** (0.07 s, ≈ 21,000 km, 5.74 / 4.32 / 1.42 million; "gap exaggerated for illustration"), small and to the side.
  - **As many frames as concepts** (C four, A and B two), plus the whole at the end; one frame may build up over 20–40 s and stay on its one thing.
  - **Voice**: the picture shows, the voice gives the why. C03 example: *(both lines grow the same orange piece)* "But in one microsecond light travels three hundred metres — every distance is overcounted by the same three hundred metres." *(beat)* "So the clock error is no small thing: it is the fourth unknown." — not "On the left is the phone, on the right two satellites" (narration that reads the screen).
- **One picture carried through**: fits one chain. With several parallel concepts, a procedure, or a real screen or organ to show, another row fits better (zoom out / zoom in gives each concept its own frame). What made it work: the script written as links, one beat each, with seams that say what stands (`04-skeleton.md` §2) and reviewed by a listener before any picture (`06-workflow.md` §1); numbers from `model.js` checked by `checks.mjs`; one colour per quantity for the whole film — a second meaning for a colour, or a figure such as "6 %" returning with a new meaning, was what the silent reviewer misread; an estimate marked ≈ with the exact value beside it; the picture answering each sentence on its word (`livecheck.py`: the first cut had 22 stretches over 3 s, the longest 10.7 s; events on the named thing brought that to 13 and 6.8 s; lines of text fading in on a still picture did not count as events on the ending card).
- **Review desk / report development / scale sandbox**: approved as middles, but their first versions had **no head and no tail**; they belong in the classroom skeleton, the default the user values.
