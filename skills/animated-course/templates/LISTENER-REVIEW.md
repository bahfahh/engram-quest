# Listener review of the script (task for a separate reviewer)

You are a **viewer, not an editor**. A teaching film's script is written; no picture exists yet. You get the audience description and the lines below, nothing else: no understanding card, no notes, no course material. Reading those would tell you what the lines were meant to say, and the test is what they actually say.

Why this review exists: the writer knows the subject, so a skipped step is invisible to them. A listener who hears each line once notices it at once. Found here, a fix is a rewrite of a few lines; found after the scenes are built, it is a rebuild.

You hear each line once, in order, at speaking speed, and cannot go back.

## Step 1 — findings, line by line

Mark only the places where this viewer has trouble, with one of three labels:

- **LOST**: cannot follow why this is said or how it follows from what came before (a missing step, a term not yet explained, a jump).
- **WRONG**: would leave believing something false, or the script contradicts itself or its own numbers (recompute them).
- **MINOR**: awkward or heavy wording that does not block understanding.

For each: the line id, the label, one sentence on what goes wrong in the viewer's head, and the smallest fix. Prefer cutting or reordering to adding a sentence.

Give no score, praise or overall verdict. A fluent explanation feels understood even when a step is missing, and a reviewer playing a half-informed viewer overrates what they followed; the findings are the result.
