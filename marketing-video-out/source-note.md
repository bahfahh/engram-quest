# Why you forget what your AI just explained

**Concept.** Reading a clear explanation feels like learning, but memory fades fastest right after you read ([[Forgetting curve]]: measured by Ebbinghaus in 1885, a similar curve found in a 2015 replication). What makes it last is pulling the answer out of your own memory ([[Retrieval practice]], also called the testing effect), and doing it again after longer and longer gaps ([[Spaced repetition]]; schedulers such as FSRS plan the gaps).

**Example.** Your AI explains how vector search finds related notes. The answer is clear and it all makes sense. A week later you ask the same question again. Instead: close the answer, say it from memory, then come back at gaps that keep growing.

**Evidence.** Roediger & Karpicke (2006): students studied short prose passages, then re-read one and, for another, closed it and wrote down what they remembered. Five minutes later re-reading did better (81% vs 75%); one week later recalling did better (56% vs 42%). In a second experiment the students who re-read the most felt surest they would remember, yet remembered less.

**Common misunderstanding.** "I understood it, so I'll remember it." Feeling that something makes sense is not evidence that you can bring it back later; re-reading feels like progress because it works right now, not next week.

Sources: Roediger & Karpicke 2006, *Psychological Science* 17(3); Murre & Dros 2015, *PLOS ONE* 10(7); Cepeda et al. 2006, *Psychological Bulletin* 132(3); FSRS, open-spaced-repetition wiki.
