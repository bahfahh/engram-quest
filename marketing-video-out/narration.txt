Your AI second brain remembers everything. Do you?

You ask your AI something hard, and the answer is crystal clear. It all makes sense. You feel like you've learned it. But a week later, you ask the exact same question. Memory fades fast at first, then slowly. Researchers measured that curve in 1885, and a 2015 replication found a similar curve.

In a classic experiment, students studied short passages. Then they re-read one passage. For another, they closed it and wrote down what they remembered. Five minutes later, re-reading won, eighty-one percent to seventy-five. But after a week, it flipped. Recalling beat re-reading, fifty-six percent to forty-two. In a second experiment, re-readers felt surest they'd remember, yet remembered less.

So after your AI explains something, close the answer, say it from memory, then come back at gaps that keep growing. Spaced repetition tools like FSRS schedule those gaps for you.

Reading feels like learning. Pulling it from memory, a little later each time, makes it stick.
